01Context and goal

A financial institution runs a collections system month after month. The goal is to estimate in advance the probability that each invoice will be paid late, to guide proactive actions and reduce default.

Business ruleA payment is considered in default when it is made 5 or more days after the due date.

Three principles that guided every decision

  1. Fidelity to the production environment. The system runs periodically and only knows the past. So every history feature is point-in-time and validation is out-of-time — train on the past, test on the future.
  2. The output is a probability, not a label. This changes technical choices: I did not rebalance the classes, I optimized a proper scoring rule (log-loss) and audited calibration.
  3. Justified simplicity. An interpretable baseline is the reference — the final model is only justified if it beats it by a wide margin.

02The data

Four tables related by ID_CLIENTE and SAFRA_REF (reference month), in latin-1 with a ; separator — reading with the wrong encoding would corrupt the categories.

Table Rows Grain Role
base_cadastral 1,315 1 per customer Static attributes: size, segment, ZIP code, area code
base_info 24,401 customer × cohort Monthly income and number of employees
base_pagamentos_dev 77,414 invoice History with payment date → generates the target
base_pagamentos_teste 12,275 invoice Recent invoices without payment → to predict

The test set is the futureDevelopment covers 2018-08 to 2021-06 (35 cohorts); the test set is 2021-07 to 2021-11 (5 cohorts), entirely later. This is a real forecast — which is exactly why validation must be temporal, never a random split. 98.1% of test customers already appear in development.

Handling missing values

Not every null is missing data. FLAG_PF is 95% missing, but per the data dictionary the absence means a legal entity — it becomes 0/1. On the other hand, RENDA_MES_ANTERIOR (2.9%), NO_FUNCIONARIOS (5.1%) and VALOR_A_PAGAR (1.5%) have genuine nulls: instead of imputing with the mean or mode, I left them as NaN — LightGBM natively learns the best side of the split, which is usually more informative. Categoricals with nulls (SEGMENTO 6.3%, DDD 18%, PORTE 3.1%, DOMINIO_EMAIL 2.3%) get their own "missing" category: missing registration data may itself carry signal.

03Building the target

The most critical step of the case. The target only exists in the development table — the only one with DATA_PAGAMENTO.

ATRASO_DIAS = DATA_PAGAMENTO − DATA_VENCIMENTO
TARGET = 1 if ATRASO_DIAS ≥ 5, else 0

Base rate: 7.02% — 5,436 defaulted invoices against 71,978 on time. A moderate imbalance, handled without resampling (see section 07).

04Exploratory analysis

Understand how the target behaves over time and by profile, to guide feature engineering.

There is a clear spike in Feb–Mar/2020, at the start of the pandemic. It is a one-off macroeconomic shock — a warning not to blindly trust calendar variables and a reinforcement that validation has to be temporal.

05Feature engineering

Four blocks of features. The behavioral history block is the most powerful — and the most dangerous.

  1. Features of the invoice itself — amount, fee, issue→due term, due month. Known at issuance, no leakage risk.
  2. Registration enrichment — company size as an ordinal variable (natural economic order), customer tenure on the invoice date, ZIP code and area code as categoricals.
  3. Monthly enrichment — join on (ID_CLIENTE, SAFRA_REF). Per the dictionary, income and number of employees refer to the end of the previous month, so they are already known at the start of the current cohort and can be used without leakage. From them I derived two financial ratios: income burden (VALOR/RENDA) and productivity (RENDA/FUNCIONÁRIOS).
  4. Point-in-time behavioral history — the anti-leakage step, detailed below.

The anti-leakage step

The credit intuition is that the best predictor of future delay is past behavior. For each invoice I want to know how many the customer has already had, their historical default rate, and average and maximum delay — using only strictly earlier cohorts. Computed naively, looking at the whole table, this block leaks the future target and inflates the result. The vectorized, safe construction:

  1. Summarize history by (customer, cohort).
  2. Accumulate over time and subtract the cohort's own value (cumsum − cohort_value), obtaining the strictly prior cumulative figure.
  3. Map back to the invoices by (customer, cohort).

For the test table — whose cohorts are all later than development — the history is the customer's total accumulated across the whole development set, which is exactly what would be available in production.

Leakage audit

  • First cohorts with non-null history: 0.0%. No customer has history at their first appearance — as it should be.
  • corr(HIST_TAXA_INADIMPLENCIA, TARGET) = 0.471. Predictive, not perfect. A correlation close to 1 would be the signature of leakage.

Final modeling table: 77,414 × 32 in development and 12,275 × 29 in test, with all test rows preserved. Ratios with a zero denominator become missing.

06Validation strategy

Out-of-time, mirroring the production scenario exactly.

Since the test set is the future of development, a random train_test_split would lie: it would mix months, leak temporal patterns and overestimate performance. I reproduced the real scenario:

Partition Cohorts Rows Default rate
Train 2018-08 → 2020-12 63,147 7.19%
Validation (OOT) 2021-01 → 2021-06 14,267 6.29%
Test (delivery) 2021-07 → 2021-11 12,275 unlabeled

The 6 validation months mirror the test horizon. The OOT validation is used both to compare models and to choose the number of trees via early stopping. The final model is then retrained on the whole development set.

07Baseline and main model

Interpretable baseline — Logistic Regression

A mandatory reference: a linear model, transparent and easy to explain. Since it handles neither NaN nor categoricals natively, I built a pipeline with imputation, scaling and one-hot encoding (rare categories grouped), using class_weight="balanced" only internally so the model learns the minority class.

Main model — LightGBM

Chosen because it handles NaN and categoricals natively — no arbitrary imputation and no one-hot explosion on CEP_2_DIG and DDD, which have high cardinality — and because it captures non-linearities and interactions that logistic regression cannot see. It is the market standard for tabular credit data.

Three decisions consistent with the goal of estimating a probability:

  1. No rebalancing. Under/oversampling distorts the predicted probability — exactly the requested output. I kept the natural ~7% rate; light regularization (min_child_samples, subsample, reg_*) handles the imbalance without bias.
  2. Early stopping on binary_logloss, not AUC. AUC only measures ranking and ignores calibration; log-loss is a proper scoring rule that rewards well-calibrated probabilities. Result: 569 trees.
  3. No post-hoc recalibration — the raw probability was already calibrated (section 08), so I added no complexity without gain.

08Evaluation and calibration

In credit risk, one metric is not enough: I evaluated ranking power (AUC, Gini, KS) and probability quality (Brier, calibration curve), plus PR-AUC, which is more informative under imbalanced classes.

Model AUC Gini KS PR-AUC Brier
Logistic Regression 0.8691 0.7382 0.6348 0.4461 0.1469
LightGBM 0.9330 0.8660 0.7374 0.6081 0.0354

LightGBM gains +0.128 Gini over the baseline and cuts the Brier score by 4× — the most relevant difference, since what is asked for is a probability.

Is the probability reliable?

The mean prediction (≈6.5%) matches the observed rate (6.3%). I also tested isotonic recalibration and the Brier score did not improve (0.0354 → 0.0354). I kept the raw probability — a decision in favor of simplicity.

The strongest features combine past behavior (HIST_*), invoice characteristics (VALOR_A_PAGAR, PRAZO_DIAS) and geography (CEP_2_DIG, DDD). Gain importance tends to favor high-cardinality variables, so in the next section I measured by ablation how much the history really contributes.

Does the model rely on shortcuts?

I removed the entire behavioral history block and retrained:

Model AUC Gini KS PR-AUC Brier
LightGBM without history 0.9205 0.8410 0.7305 0.5900 0.0369
Full LightGBM 0.9330 0.8660 0.7374 0.6081 0.0354

History adds +0.025 Gini. The important point is the inverse: even without it the model is already strong (Gini 0.841) — a sign that performance does not depend on leakage disguised as a feature.

10Final model and delivery

With the number of trees fixed by the OOT validation, I retrained LightGBM on the whole development set — making the most of the data, including the most recent cohorts, which are the most similar to the test — and generated the probabilities.

Key pointThe submission uses predict_proba — a continuous probability in [0, 1] — and not predict, which would return a 0/1 label. The case explicitly asks for the probability.

  • 12,275 rows — exactly matches the test table.
  • Columns ID_CLIENTE, SAFRA_REF, PROBABILIDADE_INADIMPLENCIA.
  • All probabilities in [0, 1], no nulls.

The mean prediction on the test set is 3.82%, below the 7.02% base rate of development. This is not a calibration error: the test population is genuinely less risky — its average historical rate is 5.60%, versus 6.58% in validation.

11Conclusions and limitations

Why the solution is solid

  • No leakage: audited point-in-time history, temporal validation that mirrors production, and an ablation showing the model does not depend on shortcuts.
  • Output faithful to the request: calibrated probability via predict_proba, with no rebalancing that would distort it.
  • Justified simplicity: a single model, no unnecessary recalibration, every decision explainable.

Limitations

  • Macro shocks — the Feb–Mar/2020 spike is not predictable from history. The model captures structural patterns, not exogenous crises.
  • Calendar variables (VENC_MES) may carry seasonality specific to one year; they require monitoring in production.
  • New customers, with no history, are predicted only from their registration and financial profile — naturally a weaker signal.

Next steps

  • Population stability (PSI) monitoring of the variables and the score.
  • Defining the cutoff based on collection cost vs. expected loss — turning probability into action.
  • Individual explainability (SHAP) to support the collections operation.
