News Classifier Served by an API

Classify the category of a Portuguese-language news article from its headline — from EDA to an API in a container. The interesting work was not the model: it was discovering that the target was not usable as it was, catching a leak that inflated the metric and choosing, with numbers in hand, to serve the weaker model.

01The problem

A Data Scientist technical case: build an end-to-end data product that classifies the category of a news article from its headline, delivered via API. The data is the public dataset news-of-the-site-folhauol — 167,053 articles from Folha de S.Paulo between Jan/2015 and Oct/2017, with headline, text, date and category.

The case's golden rule"Better something functional with a poor result than a great model with no infrastructure." This set the order of the work: close the whole flow with a simple baseline before optimizing any model.

An ambiguity in the brief, resolved in the API

The goal said to classify "the news" — which favors using the article body — but the variable list mentioned only the headline. Instead of picking one reading, the API contract serves both: title required and text optional, concatenated. If the text is not sent, the model receives only the headline and degrades in a controlled way.

02The target was not usable as it was

Two EDA findings made it unviable to use the original 48 categories directly.

22,022× imbalance

The largest category (poder) has 22,022 articles. The smallest (musica) has one. The seven largest hold about 80% of the data. Classes with 1 to 3 examples can be neither trained nor evaluated — any macro-F1 computed over them is noise.

Half the categories are not topics

Labels such as colunas, opiniao, paineldoleitor and especial reflect the newspaper's editorial organization, not the subject of the article. A column can be about politics, football or economics — training a topic classifier against that label teaches noise.

Hygiene before the split

  • 1.8% duplicate headlines (2,934 articles) removed before the train/test split. Leaving it for later would leak identical examples from one side to the other.
  • subcategory dropped as a target — 82.3% null.
  • Stratified split: 131,295 train and 32,824 test.

03From 48 categories to 12

A decision derived from the EDA, not a convenience.

I consolidated the 48 original categories into 12 topical macro-categories. The important point is that this does not change the problem: the target is still the article's category, just normalized into a coherent taxonomy. The 48→12 map is versioned in category_map.json, which makes the decision transparent and reversible.

22,022× Imbalance before poder 22,022 · musica 1
~28× After Opinion 30,158 · Career 1,057

Three groupings deserve explanation, because they came from the baseline's error analysis, not from intuition:

  • opiniao became its own Opinion class, instead of falling into Politics. Opinion is editorial, not a political topic — and the change reduced the model's biggest confusion.
  • ciencia + tec + treinamentocienciaesaude became Science and Technology: small neighboring classes that, together, form a healthy class of 3,613 examples.
  • The non-topical tail (especial, arquivo, institucional) went into an Others bucket — which, predictably, became the model's worst class for lacking cohesion. Keeping the bucket explicit is better than spreading it across the other classes.

04The leak in the coefficients

The project's most important finding — and it only appeared because I went to look at what the model had learned.

With the model trained and macro-F1 at 0.807, I inspected the LinearSVC coefficients to understand which terms weighed in each class. What I found were not topics.

Before · Economy

Terms the model used

paulo migliacci tradução paulo folhainvest grupofolha rede folha datafolha http folha

Translation credits, brand self-references and URL fragments. None of that is economics.

After · Economy

Terms after cleaning

caminhoneiros ibge contribuinte aneel

Topical vocabulary, which generalizes to articles the model has never seen.

Why TF-IDF falls for thisEditorial boilerplate is rare and predictable — it appears little in the corpus, but almost always in the same section. A rare term gets a high IDF, and the model finds a perfect shortcut. The problem is that train and test share the same boilerplate: the metric rises while the ability to generalize falls. Without inspecting the coefficients, the number would have passed as good.

Two measured mitigations, one chosen

Approach Macro-F1 Cost Side effect
No mitigation (contaminated) 0.807 — Metric inflated by shortcuts
Blocklist + URL and credits cleanup 0.800 −0.007 Zeroes out boilerplate in the top-40 coefficients
Truncate to the lead (400 characters) 0.745 −0.062 Also zeroes it out, and makes the vocabulary 6× smaller

I chose the blocklist: seven thousandths of F1 is a cheap price for an honest number. Truncating to the lead solves it equally, but costs 62 thousandths — it is kept as a deployment lever, in case vocabulary size becomes a constraint.

Single source of truthThe cleaning lives in src/preprocessing.py and is called by both training and the API. If it existed only in the training notebook, the API would receive text processed differently — the classic train/serve skew, where the model in production performs worse than on paper and nobody understands why.

05Models and production decision

Same split, same metric, three experiments.

Macro-F1 on the holdout

32,824 articles, 12 macro-categories.

0.668
TF-IDF + LinearSVCtitle + text — production
0.800
BERTimbaufine-tuned, title + text
0.858

I also tested LogisticRegression, MultinomialNB and MLPClassifier. The MLP reached 0.585 in the title-only scenario, below the linear models and slower — discarded. LinearSVC with class_weight="balanced" won in cross-validation. Preprocessing hyperparameters were treated as real hyperparameters, via GridSearchCV, and not as fixed truths: removing stopwords helped in the title+text scenario (min_df=5) and hurt in title-only (min_df=2), because a short headline has no words to spare.

BERT wins. And still it is not the default.

Model Macro-F1 Latency/req Size Dependencies
TF-IDF + LinearSVC (default) 0.800 0.68 ms 132 MB scikit-learn
BERTimbau (on demand) 0.858 100–300 ms 440 MB ~2.5 GB

BERTimbau delivers +5.75 points of macro-F1. It costs, in exchange, about 300× more latency and 20× more dependency weight. For a news classification API, the decision order I fixed before seeing the numbers — quality → latency → size — leads to LinearSVC.

But discarding BERT would also waste the work. The API serves both in the same container: svc by default, bert when the caller prioritizes accuracy and accepts the cost. The right choice comes out of the box, without taking the option away from whoever needs the other.

06The API

FastAPI with GET /health, POST /predict and Swagger at /docs. 

POST /predict
{
  "title": "Palmeiras vence o clássico e assume a liderança",
  "explicar": true
}
{
  "categoria": "Esporte",
  "confianca": 0.32,
  "top3": [
    {"categoria": "Esporte", "confianca": 0.32},
    {"categoria": "Cultura e Entretenimento", "confianca": 0.09},
    {"categoria": "Opinião", "confianca": 0.09}
  ],
  "palavras_influentes": [
    {"palavra": "vence", "peso": 0.74},
    {"palavra": "palmeiras", "peso": 0.31}
  ]
}

Field names and category labels are returned in Portuguese, as in the source dataset.

Explainability without a library

The palavras_influentes field shows what weighed in the decision. Since the model is linear, each term's contribution is tfidf × coefficient — an exact value, not an approximation. It is a case where LIME or SHAP would be extra work for less precision. It works as an argument in favor of the simple model: it explains its own decision for free.

Details that avoid surprises

  • Confidence is a score, not a probability. LinearSVC has no predict_proba; the value is the softmax of the decision_function and is documented in the schema as a relative score — not a calibrated probability.
  • Validation in the schema, not the endpoint. A Pydantic field_validator requires a headline of at least 3 words and returns HTTP 422 with a clear message. Because it lives in the schema, the rule shows up in Swagger on its own.
  • BERT with lazy loading. It only loads on the first call that asks for it, and uses a GPU if available. Anyone who never requests bert does not pay the cost of having it in the container.
  • Explicit degradation without text. The 0.80 F1 holds when the body is sent; with the headline only, performance drops to the 0.67 range. It is documented, rather than becoming a production surprise.

07Docker and reproducibility

docker compose up -d     # starts the API
# http://127.0.0.1:8000/docs

On a clean clone, with no prerequisites at all: no need to train anything or download the 500 MB dataset. Both models are published on the Hugging Face Hub and downloaded during the build, staying in the image cache — so there is no download at runtime.

  • No weights versioned in Git. The SVC is 132 MB and BERT is 440 MB — above GitHub's 100 MB limit. They live on the Hub; the repository holds only code.
  • Torch in the +cpu variant. The CUDA build would pull in about 2 GB unnecessarily. On a host with a GPU and the NVIDIA toolkit, BERT uses the card anyway.
  • Model regenerable from code. python src/train.py rebuilds the production artifact from scratch — the .joblib does not need to exist for the project to be reproducible.
  • Healthcheck marks the container as healthy when the baseline finishes loading, in about 5 seconds.

08Limitations

  • The label is Folha's section, with all the editorial bias that carries. The model learns one newspaper's taxonomy, not a universal truth about news categories.
  • Data from 2015 to 2017. Political, economic and technological vocabulary ages fast; in real use, the model would need periodic retraining and drift monitoring.
  • The Others class is a bucket and remains the model's worst. It has no topical cohesion — by construction.
  • Confidence is not calibrated. It serves to rank, not to make threshold-based decisions. Calibrating would require CalibratedClassifierCV or equivalent.
  • Uncatalogued boilerplate remains. The blocklist zeroed out what appeared in the top-40 coefficients; there is no guarantee that nothing else exists below that.
